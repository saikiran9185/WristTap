WristTap · build guide · v1 · 14 Nov 2026

A sensor wristband that detects mid-air pinches and taps without a camera

Follow the pages in order. Each step ends with a check: don't move on until the check passes. If something fails, go to the troubleshooting page.

What it does

Sensors on your wrist pick up the small vibration and tendon movement made when your thumb touches a fingertip. While you record, the Mac camera labels each pinch automatically. Later a model learns to detect pinches from the band alone, with the camera off.

What's already done on the Mac

✓arduino-cli + ESP32 support installedUsed to upload code to the board. You don't need the Arduino app.
✓Firmware compilesfirmware/wristtap/wristtap.ino, 23% of the board's memory
✓Mac recorder testedwristtap.py handled 999 fake packets, caught the 1 dropped packet, ignored noise
✓One-click scripts./flash.sh uploads the code · ./check.sh shows live sensor values · ./record.sh records a session

All files are in ~/Projects/WristTap.

Wiring reference

Every wire, in one table

Pin names are printed on the ESP32 board. D21 may also be printed as G21 or IO21. VP may be printed as SVP (GPIO36), and VN as SVN (GPIO39).

SensorSensor pin / wireGoes toNotes
IMU 1 (wrist)VCC3V3Never VIN or 5V
GNDGND
SDAD21Shared with IMU 2
SCLD22Shared with IMU 2
AD0GNDGives address 0x68
IMU 2 (hand)VCC3V3
GNDGND
SDAD21Same breadboard row as IMU 1's SDA
SCLD22Same breadboard row as IMU 1's SCL
AD03V3Must be 3V3, or both IMUs get the same address
Piezo 0 / 1 / 2 / 3Red wire10 kΩ → D32 / D33 / D34 / D35The 10 kΩ protects the pin from voltage spikes
Black wireGNDAlso add a 1 MΩ from the D-pin to GND for each piezo
FSR 0 / 1Leg A3V3FSRs have no polarity; either leg works
Leg BVP / VNAlso add a 10 kΩ from VP (or VN) to GND
INT, XDA, XCLLeave unconnected on both IMUs.

How to use the breadboard

Don't push the ESP32 into the breadboard. It's too wide and covers almost every hole. Leave it loose and connect to its pins with female–male jumpers.
Power railsOne jumper from ESP32 3V3 to the breadboard's red (+) rail, one from ESP32 GND to the blue (−) rail. Every "3V3" and "GND" in the table above means these rails.
One row per signalGive each ESP32 signal pin its own breadboard row (D21, D22, D32, D33, D34, D35, VP, VN = 8 rows), with a jumper from that row to the ESP32 pin.
Sensors join the rowThe sensor wire goes into the same row. For example, the D21 row holds: IMU 1 SDA, IMU 2 SDA and the jumper to ESP32 D21.
Resistors to groundThe 1 MΩ (piezo rows) or 10 kΩ (FSR rows) goes from that row to the blue GND rail. The piezo's 10 kΩ sits between the piezo's red wire and the row.
Label the rowsWrite the pin name next to each row on a strip of tape. It's the easiest way to avoid wiring mistakes.
Rule: build with the USB cable unplugged. Plug in only after you've checked every new wire against this table.

Build · one sensor at a time

Six steps, each with a check

1

ESP32 alone: upload the code

  1. Keep the ESP32 loose, not in the breadboard (see page 3). Plug it into the Mac.
  2. Open Terminal and run:
cd ~/Projects/WristTap && ./flash.sh

If it stalls at Connecting...., hold the BOOT button on the ESP32 until writing starts.

./check.sh
Check: about 500 pkt/s, drops 0, both IMUs show MISSING. That's correct for now.
2

IMU 1

Unplug USB. Run 3V3 and GND wires to the breadboard rails. Wire IMU 1 using the table on page 3 (AD0 → GND). Plug in, then run ./check.sh.

Check: IMU1 |a| ≈ 1.00 g when it's lying still (that's gravity). Shake it and the peak goes up.
3

IMU 2

Unplug. Wire IMU 2 with AD0 → 3V3. Press the EN (reset) button after plugging in, because the IMUs are only detected at startup.

Check: IMU1 and IMU2 both ≈ 1.00 g. Move only one: only its peak should change.
4

Piezos (one at a time)

Piezo 0: red → 10 kΩ → D32, black → GND, 1 MΩ from D32 to GND. Then check, then piezo 1 on D33, piezo 2 on D34, piezo 3 on D35.

Check: that piezo's number jumps clearly when you tap it lightly with a fingernail, and stays low when you don't.
5

FSRs

FSR 0: one leg → 3V3, other leg → VP, 10 kΩ from VP to GND. FSR 1 on VN, the same way.

Check: near 0 when untouched, rising to 1000–4000 as you press harder.
6

Mount on your wrist (use your dominant hand)

  1. Inside the wrist band, against the skin: FSR 0 at the centre of the inner wrist (palm side), FSR 1 toward the pinky side. Piezo 0 on the thumb side, piezo 1 on the pinky side, piezo 2 under IMU 1, piezo 3 next to FSR 0. Hold them in place with foam tape.
  2. On top of the band: IMU 1, like a watch face. Back of the hand: IMU 2, held with insulation tape.
  3. ESP32 + breadboard on the forearm strap. Fix the wires with zip ties so they don't pull on the sensors.
  4. Snug, not tight: the band shouldn't slide, but your fingers must stay comfortable.
  5. Take a photo of where everything sits, so every session uses the same placement.
Check: ./check.sh still shows all sensors. Pinch thumb to index firmly: at least one piezo or FSR should react.

Record the dataset

The camera labels, the band learns

Each session cues 5 gestures in random order. The Mac camera measures thumb-to-fingertip distance in every frame, which gives an automatic label for training the band.

Setup

  1. Sit at the desk, elbow on the table, with your band hand clearly visible to the Mac camera (palm facing the camera works best). Good light.
  2. The first time, macOS will ask to allow the camera for Terminal: click Allow.
cd ~/Projects/WristTap && ./record.sh --reps 20 --note "session 1, right wrist"

Press SPACE to start. Each cue shows "next: …" for 1.2 s, then "NOW: …" for 1.5 s. Do the gesture once, then relax. Press Q to stop early. One session takes about 5 minutes.

GestureWhat to do when you see NOW
RESTKeep your hand still and relaxed
PINCH INDEXTap the thumb against the index fingertip once, firmly, then open
PINCH MIDDLESame, with the middle finger
PINCH PINKYSame, with the pinky
AIR TAPFlick the index finger down in the air once, as if tapping a glass wall

Plan for a clean dataset

Session 120 reps × 5 gestures. Band on, sitting.
Session 2Take the band off, put it back on, record again. This tests whether the model survives re-wearing.
Session 3A different hand angle (palm down). This tests robustness.
Optional1–2 friends, one session each. More people makes the result much more convincing.

Sessions are saved in ~/Projects/WristTap/sessions/. Next we train a model in PyTorch and score it leave-one-session-out, the same honest method you used in the sign study.

What counts as a result: "Detected pinch vs rest X% of the time, which finger Y%, latency Z ms, on sessions it never saw." Even a modest number is a real result if it's measured honestly. If "which finger" fails, that's still a finding: report it and explain why.
Privacy: the recorder saves only numbers (sensor values and finger distances), never camera images.

Troubleshooting + safety

If something goes wrong

ProblemMost likely causeFix
flash.sh: "No ESP32 port found"Charge-only cable, or a missing USB driverTry the other cable or USB port. If it's still missing, tell Claude the chip name (CP2102 or CH340) to install its driver.
Upload stuck at "Connecting…"Board not in upload modeHold BOOT while it connects. Release once it shows "Writing".
check.sh: "no packets yet"Firmware not runningPress EN. Make sure only one program is using the port (close other terminals running check or record).
IMU shows MISSINGSDA/SCL swapped, loose wire, or VCC not on 3V3Unplug and re-check against page 3. Then press EN, because IMUs are only detected at startup.
IMU 2 MISSING but IMU 1 worksAD0 of IMU 2 not on 3V3Move IMU 2's AD0 wire to the 3V3 rail, then press EN.
IMU |a| reads 0 or ≫ 1 g at restFaulty moduleSwap in the spare MPU6050.
Piezo always about 4095Missing 1 MΩ to GND (the pin is floating)Add the 1 MΩ from the D-pin to GND.
Piezo never reactsRed wire on the wrong pin, or the disc isn't pressed against anythingCheck the pin. Tap the disc directly. On the wrist it must be pressed firmly against the skin.
FSR always about 4095Missing 10 kΩ to GNDAdd the 10 kΩ from VP/VN to GND.
FSR always 0Leg not on 3V3, or a loose wireFSR legs are thin. Use a female jumper on each leg and wrap it with tape.
"drops" keep climbingLoose USB connection or a cheap hubPlug straight into the Mac. Don't use a hub.
Board resets / gets hotShort circuit: 3V3 touching GNDUnplug immediately. Look for bare wires touching each other.
Camera: no hand detectedHand out of frame, or poor lightMove closer, face your palm to the camera, turn on a lamp.

Safety rules

  1. Never connect 5V or VIN to any sensor or to D-pins. Use 3V3 only.
  2. Change wiring only with the USB cable unplugged.
  3. Don't hit the piezos hard while they're connected: a strong hit can make a voltage spike. The 10 kΩ resistors are there to protect the pins.
  4. Keep exposed metal (resistor legs, header pins) off your skin. Insulation tape over the FSR legs is enough.
Stuck? Run ./check.sh, take a phone photo of the wiring, and send both to Claude.
