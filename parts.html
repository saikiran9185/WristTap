What each part looks like, and why you need it

Each part has a CRITICAL or NEEDED tag. Without a CRITICAL part the band can't work at all. The green box tells you how to recognise the right one in the shop.

ESP32 DevKit V1 (WROOM-32, 30-pin)CRITICAL×2 · ₹350–450 each
What it does: the brain. It reads every sensor 500 times a second and sends the data to your Mac over USB.
Why ×2: if one is faulty or gets damaged by a wiring mistake, the spare saves the day.
Silver metal box on one end, micro-USB port on the other, 2 small buttons (EN, BOOT), 15 pins per side. Ask for the CP2102 chip version.
MPU6050 module (GY-521)CRITICAL×3 · ₹120–150 each
What it does: an IMU, which measures movement and rotation. One on the wrist, one on the back of the hand. A pinch makes a tiny jolt they can feel.
Why it matters: this is the "IMU" in KAIST's Project #1 setup. ×3 = 2 used + 1 spare.
Small blue board, black square chip in the middle, 8 pins labelled VCC GND SCL SDA XDA XCL AD0 INT.
Piezo disc 27 mm, wires attachedCRITICAL×5 · ₹10–20 each
What it does: a vibration sensor. Pressed against your skin, it picks up the tiny "click" that travels up the wrist when your thumb meets a fingertip.
Why it matters: the cheapest and probably the most useful sensor for detecting when a tap happens. ×5 = 4 used + 1 spare.
Gold/brass disc with a white or grey centre. Get ones with wires already soldered. Ask the shop to add a pin on each wire end.
FSR 402 (force sensing resistor)CRITICAL×2 · ₹250–400 each
What it does: measures pressure. Inside the band it feels your wrist tendons bulge when a finger moves, a method called force myography.
Why it matters: this is the "FSR" in KAIST's setup, and it helps tell which finger pinched.
Thin flexible round pad on a flat tail with 2 metal legs. Not the square one, not the long strip.

Connecting parts
Resistors: 1 MΩ and 10 kΩCRITICAL×10 each · ~₹1 each
1 MΩ: drains the piezo so its reading settles back to zero after each tap. Without it the reading sticks at maximum.
10 kΩ: (a) protects the ESP32 pins from piezo voltage spikes, (b) turns the FSR's pressure into a voltage the ESP32 can read.
The only difference is the 3rd colour band: GREEN = 1 MΩ, ORANGE = 10 kΩ. Keep them in two labelled bags so they don't get mixed up.
Jumper wires (3 kinds)CRITICALF–F ×40, M–F ×40, M–M ×20 · ~₹160
What they do: connect everything without soldering.
Female–male (20 cm) goes from the ESP32 and sensor pins to the breadboard. You'll use these the most. Female–female joins pin to pin. Male–male (10 cm) is for short links on the breadboard.
Usually sold as a joined ribbon of 40 that you peel apart. Check both ends of each kind before paying.
Breadboard, 400 point (half size)CRITICAL×1 · ~₹80
What it does: a board full of connected holes. The long red and blue rails share power (3V3) and ground with every sensor, and the resistors plug in here.
Remember: the ESP32 does not go into it, because it's too wide. It stays loose and connects with jumpers.
White plastic, red and blue lines along both long edges, a groove down the middle.
USB data cable (micro-USB, 1 m)CRITICAL×1 · ~₹100
What it does: powers the ESP32, uploads the code and carries all the sensor data to the Mac.
The #1 beginner mistake: many cheap cables only charge and carry no data. The Mac then never sees the board.
Test it in the shop: plug it into your phone. If the phone asks "File transfer / USB options", it's a data cable. Check the ESP32's port too: micro-USB (most) or USB-C.
USB-C to USB-A adapterONLY IF NEEDED×1 · ~₹100
What it does: newer Macs only have USB-C ports, and the ESP32 cable has the big USB-A end.
Skip it if your Mac has a rectangular USB-A port, or you buy a USB-C to micro-USB cable instead.
Plug straight into the Mac, not through a hub: hubs cause data dropouts.

Wearing it + optional extras
Elastic velcro strap, 2.5 cm wideCRITICAL×2 · ~₹50 each
What it does: strap 1 is the wristband and presses the piezos and FSRs against your skin. Strap 2 holds the ESP32 and breadboard on your forearm.
Why elastic: the sensors need steady, even pressure on the skin. Too loose and they pick up nothing, too tight and it's uncomfortable.
Stretchy, with velcro, about 2.5 cm wide and at least 25 cm long. Watch-strap or bag shops also have these.
Foam tape, insulation tape, small zip tiesNEEDED1 each · ~₹100 total
Foam tape (double-sided): sticks the sensors inside the band. The foam also helps press them onto the skin.
Insulation tape: holds IMU 2 on the back of your hand and covers bare metal legs. Zip ties: keep wires from pulling on the sensors.
Optional, only if your budget allows
EMG muscle sensor + gel electrodesOPTIONAL₹1,500–2,500
What it does: reads the electrical signal of your forearm muscles. It's the "EMG" in KAIST's Project #1, so it's the closest match to their setup.
Why optional: costly, needs fresh sticky pads every session, and the code needs a small update first. Build the main band first.
Soldering iron kit · multimeterOPTIONAL₹300–500 · ₹250–400
Soldering iron (25 W + solder + stand): not needed today if the shop solders the pins for you, but useful for every future hardware project.
Multimeter: checks whether a wire is actually connected. It makes troubleshooting much faster.

Total for CRITICAL + NEEDED: about ₹2,000–2,600

At the counter, before you pay: (1) "Header pins solder kar do" on both ESP32s and all 3 MPU6050s. (2) Solder a pin onto each piezo wire end. (3) Plug the ESP32 into a charger: the red LED must light. (4) Test the USB cable on your phone for file transfer.
